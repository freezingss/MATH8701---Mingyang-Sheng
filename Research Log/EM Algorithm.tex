\section{A Framework for Poisson Structure}
Let $\bm{y}_{ij} \in \mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^Q$ denote the count vector for observation $i$ in group $j(i)$ with $Q$ categories. For each observation $i$, we have an injection mapping $j(i) \in \{1, 2, \ldots, J\}$. Define $\mathcal{I}_j = \{i : j(i) = j\}$ and $I_j = |\mathcal{I}_j|$, so that the total number of observations satisfies $I = \sum_{j=1}^J I_j$. Conditional on the multinomial sums $y_{ij+}=\sum_{q=1}^{Q}y_{ijq}$ and vector-valued random effect $\bm{\lambda}_{j}=\bigl(\lambda_{jq}\bigr)_{q=1}^{Q}$ for group $j$, the counts $\bm{y}_{ij}$ are independently multinomially distributed for each observation $i$, i.e.
\begin{equation}
    \bm{y}_{ij} \mid  y_{ij+} , \bm{\lambda}_{ij}
    \sim \operatorname{Multinomial}\left(y_{ij+},\, \bm{p}_{ij}\right),
\end{equation}
Given the random effects, we treat $y_{ij+}$ as random and assume
\begin{equation}
    y_{ij+}\mid \bm{\lambda}_{j}\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\delta_{ij}\sum_{q=1}^{Q}\lambda_{jq}\zeta_{ijq}),
\end{equation}
independently for each $i$ and $j$. This gives\cite{lee2017poissontrickextensionsfitting} 
\begin{equation}
    y_{ijq}\mid \lambda_{jq}\sim \operatorname{Poisson}(\delta_{ij}\lambda_{jq}\zeta_{ijq}),
\end{equation}
independently for each $i$ and $q$. Random effects are unobserved naturally, thus \textcite{lee2017poissontrickextensionsfitting} obtained the marginal distribution by integrating out the random effects and expressed the closed-form of the marginal likelihood of the approximating Poisson surrogate model with the assumption for the gamma distribution of the random effects.
Our approach takes a fundamentally different route rather than marginalizing out the random effects. We explicitly decompose the random effects through a factor analyzer
\begin{equation}
    \log\boldsymbol{\lambda}_j = \boldsymbol{\mu} + \mathbf{B}\mathbf{f}_j 
    + \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_j, \quad 
    \mathbf{f}_j \sim \mathcal{N}_K(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{I}_K), \quad
    \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_j \sim \mathcal{N}_Q(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{D})
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{B} \in \mathbb{R}^{Q \times K}$ is the factor loading matrix and $\mathbf{D} = \mathrm{diag}(d_1^2, \ldots, d_Q^2)$. Since $\mathbf{f}_j$ is unobserved, the marginal distribution of $y_{ijq}$ given $\mathbf{f}_j$ requires integrating out $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_j$, which does not admit a closed form. This motivates the Laplace approximation in the E-step.
